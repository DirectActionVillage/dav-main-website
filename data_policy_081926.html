Data Policy

Last updated October 15, 2026

This page covers what data our website and forms collect, who can see that data, and what eventually happens to that data. We don't have lawyers, so this isn't a legal privacy policy. If something looks wrong, tell us and we'll try to fix it!

This website

This website doesn't run JavaScript or set cookies. You can check for yourself with right-click + Inspect, or with uBlock Origin. That said, GitHub hosts this site, so GitHub's servers see your IP address and the date/time stamps that you visit this page and all of its sub-pages (like any other web host). If that is a problem for you, consider using a reputable VPN (ideally one that is open-source and passes independent security audits).

GitHub (a US company) never sees what you type into our signup or grant forms, because neither form is on this site. They live on news.directactionvillage.org, which GitHub doesn't host.

What we collect

Our mailing list signup only asks for a name and an email. The name can be anything (it doesn't have to be your legal name). Any email works, but we'd rather it didn't give away who you are (ideally not firstname.lastname@provider.com). Even better, use a privacy-respecting provider like Tuta, RiseUp, or Proton. If all you have is firstname.lastname@gmail.com, that's fine, and we'll still do our best to protect you.

The signup page runs one small script. It's a captcha that has your browser solve a quick puzzle, so bots can't sign up in bulk. We host it ourselves, so it doesn't call out to any other company. After you sign up, we email you a link to confirm. You won't get any newsletters until you click the link.

We don't keep the IP address you signed up from. The newsletter software is set not to record it, and the server in front of the form doesn't keep access logs. Our emails don't have tracking either, so we can't tell whether you opened one or clicked a link.

Our grant application form only requires the questions about your project. Everything about you (name, email, phone, and Signal username) is optional, so you can apply without any contact info. We just won't be able to tell you if you win! The grant form doesn't run any JavaScript. We'll delete applications 7 days after we announce that round's decisions, or sooner if you ask. To slow down spam, the grant form holds an unreadable, keyed form of your IP address in memory for up to an hour, then forgets it.

Where your data lives

The mailing list lives on machines we own and run ourselves (not a cloud service), and so do our backups. The disks are encrypted, so a stolen disk can't be read. One server we rent does hold your email for a while (more on that below).

The grant form locks (encrypts) each application the moment it arrives. Only the volunteers who handle applications hold the keys to unlock it. The keys aren't on the server, so the server and the newsletter can't read applications. They open each application on their own computer. Then they pass it on to the grant committee for its monthly meeting (printed out, read aloud, or over Signal). Nobody else holds a key.

Deleting your data isn't instant. Copies of your records sit in our backups until those roll over. We keep 7 daily backups and 4 weekly ones. Our storage server also keeps a week of snapshots of those, in case a backup gets damaged. The two newest backups go to an offsite drive too, and we refresh it at least every five weeks. So the oldest copy is usually about a month old, and at most about seven weeks.

Who sees your data

We run and send our own newsletter. If you sign up with the form, your data doesn't pass through a mailing company or any other third-party service. A few machines that aren't ours still see parts of it, though.

A server we rent, in a non-US country covered by GDPR, handles the mailing list. That server:

  • Holds the certificate for news.directactionvillage.org (so it sees what you type into the signup and grant forms before passing it on to our machines).
  • Sends the newsletter (so it sees your email if you're on the list).
  • Holds bounce notices when an email can't reach you, until our machines pick them up (usually within minutes). Those include your email too.

Proton is our email provider. If you email us at directactionvillage@proton.me instead of using the form, Proton sees your name and email. It also sees any reply we send you. Your own email provider sees every email we send you too, and that part is out of our hands.

Unsubscribing and downloading/deleting your data

Every newsletter has a one-click unsubscribe link. The same link opens a page where you can see what we have on you, download it, and delete it yourself, without asking anyone. If you'd rather talk to a person, email us at directactionvillage@proton.me.

What we can and can't prove

This website's code is public on our GitHub, and so is the code that handles mailing list data. You still can't check that the published code is what's actually running on our machines. Nobody who runs an online service for you can prove that! So we're asking you to trust us, but nothing we can say or show you is a guarantee. In the end, you still need to make your own calls about your own data.
